# sfe — Terms of Use

## What sfe is and is not

sfe is a pilot service, run by [legal name of the company] ("we"), that lets you talk by voice with a vetted volunteer when you feel unsafe or want someone to talk to. By using sfe you agree to these terms.

**sfe is not an emergency service.** It does not send police, ambulances or any other help to you. If you or anyone else is in danger or needs medical help, call 112 first.

Protectors are volunteers. They are not police officers, doctors, counsellors or lawyers, and they cannot give professional advice.

## Who can use sfe and how

You must be 18 or older. Give accurate details, keep your password private, and tell us if someone else may have access to your account.

Use sfe only for real situations. Please do not:

- send false alerts or test it without agreeing this with us (people taking part in the pilot are welcome);
- abuse, threaten, harass or make sexual comments to a protector. Protectors may end any call;
- record a call or share what a protector says;
- try to find, contact or meet a protector outside the app;
- use sfe for anything illegal or to harm anyone.

To prevent misuse, each account can send a limited number of alerts per hour (currently 5). We may suspend or remove accounts that break these rules.

## No guarantee of an answer

A protector can answer only if one is online and free. Sometimes nobody will. If nobody answers within [30] seconds, or none is online, the app tells you at once and shows the 112 button.

sfe may not work in every location or circumstance, for example without an internet connection, with the microphone blocked, or when your phone is locked or in a poor signal area. Never rely on sfe alone.

If you or anyone else is in danger or needs medical help, call **112**. In Spain you can also call the Policía Nacional on 091 or the Guardia Civil on 062. A protector may tell you to call 112, and you should.

## Protectors

Protectors are unpaid volunteers. Before taking calls they complete identity verification, a criminal-record declaration, two reference checks and training. These checks reduce risk but cannot remove it, and we cannot guarantee any protector's judgment or advice.

Protectors speak only by voice. They never come to you, never ask for your phone number, address or money, and never speak for the police or any authority. If a protector does any of these things, end the call, call 112 if you are in danger, and report it in the app.

After a call you can rate the protector and report a problem. Our safety team reviews every report.
